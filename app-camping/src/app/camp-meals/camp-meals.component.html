🍳 Camp Cooking & Meals

🎉 Introduction: Cooking is a Core Scout Skill

🎯 One of the main purposes of Bootcamp is learning how to prepare and cook meals as a patrol. This is a hands-on skill you'll use on every future campout — so pay attention, ask questions, and get involved!

📋 How Meals Work at Bootcamp:

  • 🔥 Your patrol plans and cooks its own meals. Patrols have already discussed and planned their food for the trip. Youth leaders will teach new Scouts how to use the two-burner propane camp stoves the Troop provides.
  • 🍫 Personal snacks are fine — but see the rules below for where they may be stored and eaten.
  • 💧 Stay hydrated: Bring a reusable water bottle. Drink water throughout the day, especially during activities and hikes.

🌲 Key Principle: Leave No Trace — pack out all food wrappers, leftover food, and trash. We leave the campsite better than we found it.

 🔥 Part 1: Patrol Cooking

🍽️ Your Patrol Plans the Menu: Each patrol has already worked out its meal plan for the trip. If you have questions about what your patrol is cooking, talk to your Patrol Leader.

🔥 How Camp Cooking Works:

  • The Troop provides two-burner propane camp stoves.
  • Youth leaders will instruct new Scouts on how to safely set up, light, and use the stoves.
  • Every Scout participates — in preparing, cooking, and cleaning up. This is not a spectator activity!
  • Cooking together as a patrol is one of the best parts of camping. Embrace it!

🥜 Food Allergies & Dietary Concerns: Patrols have already discussed allergies and dietary needs within the patrol. If you have a concern about your patrol's food plan, contact your Patrol Leader or the Senior Patrol Leader before the trip.

 🍫 Part 2: Personal Snacks

🍫 Personal snacks are allowed, but there are rules about where they can be kept and eaten:

  • ❌ No food or snacks inside tents — ever. This keeps the campsite clean and critter-free.
  • ❌ No sweet-smelling items in tents either — this includes gum, lip balm, and toothpaste.
  • ✅ Talk to your Senior Patrol Leader about where to store your personal snacks while at camp.

🍫 Snack Ideas:

  • Granola bars, energy bars
  • Trail mix, nuts, dried fruit
  • Crackers and peanut butter packets
  • Fruit snacks, jerky 🥩

💡 Pack snacks in a labeled zip-top bag inside your daypack — easy to access during activities, easy to keep organized.

 🍴 Part 3: Your Mess Kit

✅ What to Bring:

  • 🍽️ Plate and bowl: A camp mess kit, plastic plates/bowls, or a sturdy reusable set. No need for fancy gear.
  • 🥄 Fork, spoon, and knife: A camp spork or basic utensil set works great. Pack them in a zip-top bag.
  • ☕ Cup or mug: For hot chocolate or warm drinks. An insulated mug keeps drinks warm.
  • 💧 Water bottle: A reusable water bottle (at least 1 liter). You'll fill it at the camp water spigot.

❌ Don't Overthink It: Any plates, bowls, and utensils from home will work fine. Just make sure everything is labeled with your name and can handle being washed at camp.

🧼 Cleanup: After meals, Scouts wash their own mess kits at the designated wash station. Youth leaders will show you the proper technique (wash, rinse, sanitize).

 🌲 Part 4: Food Safety & Leave No Trace

🍽️ Food Storage: Keep food tidy and put away when not in use:

  • Never leave food out unattended. Keep food in sealed containers or coolers when not eating.
  • Do not keep food or sweet-smelling items in your tent overnight. Ask your Senior Patrol Leader where to store food.

🗑️ Leave No Trace:

  • Pack out ALL trash — every wrapper, bag, and scrap of food.
  • Use the trash bags provided at camp. If in doubt, pick it up!
  • "Micro-trash" (tiny bits of wrappers or food) counts too. Inspect your area before leaving.

🧴 Hand Hygiene:

  • Wash your hands (or use hand sanitizer) before eating and after using the bathroom.
  • Hand sanitizer is your friend at camp — use it liberally!
