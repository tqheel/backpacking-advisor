⛺ Gear & What to Pack

🎯 Introduction: Car Camping is Forgiving!

🎉 The Good News: Gear travels to camp in the Troop trailer — you don't hike in with it on your back! That said, everything you bring must fit in one duffel bag or large backpack plus a small daypack. Pack smart and you'll be comfortable all weekend.

🎒 Scouts: Packing is YOUR job. Use this guide to pack your own gear. Don't ask a parent to do it for you — part of being a Scout is learning to take care of yourself. Go through this guide, check off the checklist, and pack your bag yourself. You'll be proud you did.

🎯 The Goal: Be comfortable, warm, dry, and prepared. A miserable first camping trip is a Scout who never comes back — so pack smart and stay comfortable!

📋 The Big Picture:

  • 😴 Sleeping Setup: Tent, sleeping bag, pad, camp pillow — sleep well and you'll love camping.
  • 👕 Clothing: Layers are key. Spring nights at Camp Reeves can still be cold!
  • 🔦 Camp Essentials: Headlamp, first aid, pocket knife, sunscreen, and bug spray.
  • 📚 Scout Gear: Handbook and any rank materials for advancement activities.
 😴 Part 1: Sleeping Setup

⛺ Tent:

  • The Troop has a limited number of 2-person tents available to borrow. Contact our Quartermaster, Mr. Woody, if you'd like to borrow one.
  • You may bring your own tent, but it must sleep no more than 2 people. Larger tents take up too much trailer and campsite space and take too long to set up and tear down.
  • Scouts help set up and take down their own tent — leaders will teach first-timers!

🛏️ Sleeping Bag:

  • A sleeping bag rated to 20–30°F is recommended — the same rating as our backpacking trips. Spring nights at Camp Reeves can dip into the 30s°F.
  • Don't have one? A regular sleeping bag plus an extra blanket works in a pinch.
  • Keep your sleeping bag in a stuff sack or compression bag so it fits in your duffel.

🟦 Sleeping Pad:

  • A sleeping pad insulates you from the cold ground — this matters even more than the sleeping bag!
  • Recommended for new Scouts: a closed-cell foam pad. They're cheap, durable, and roll up small.
  • A self-inflating pad (one you inflate with your breath) is also fine.
  • ❌ Full-size air mattresses that require a pump are not appropriate — they take up too much space in the trailer and tent.

🪨 Pillow: Bring a small camp pillow — the kind that compresses into a stuff sack. Full-size bed pillows are not appropriate for troop trips; they're too bulky for the trailer and tent.

 👕 Part 2: Clothing & Layers

🌸 Spring at Camp Reeves: Days can be warm but mornings and nights still get cold (30s–50s°F). Layers are your best friend.

🧥 Layering System:

  • Base Layer: A moisture-wicking shirt and long underwear (synthetics or wool are best, but cotton is okay for camp activities — just avoid cotton for any hikes).
  • Mid Layer: A fleece jacket or hoodie for warmth.
  • Shell Layer: A rain jacket or waterproof shell. Rain pants are a bonus. Weather in NC can change quickly!

👔 Suggested Clothing List:

  • 2–3 T-shirts or long-sleeve shirts
  • 1 pair of pants (jeans are okay, but quick-dry pants are better) + 1 pair of shorts
  • 2–3 pairs of socks (wool or synthetic preferred; bring extras — wet socks make for a miserable trip)
  • Sturdy, closed-toe shoes or hiking boots 🥾 — no flip-flops or open-toed shoes of any kind. Ever.
  • Fleece jacket or hoodie 🧥
  • Rain jacket 🌧️ (essential!)
  • Warm hat (beanie) 🧢 and gloves 🧤 for cold mornings
  • Pajamas or dedicated sleep clothes (keep them separate and dry)
  • Underwear (bring an extra pair!)

💡 Pro Tip: Stuff each day's outfit into a separate zip-top bag. Getting dressed in a dark tent is much easier when your clothes are organized!

 🔦 Part 3: Camp Essentials

✅ Must-Have Items:

  • 💡 Headlamp + extra batteries 🔋. Hands-free light is essential after dark. A headlamp beats a flashlight every time.
  • 🩹 Personal First Aid: Band-aids, moleskin for blisters, any personal medications. Give prescription medications to your Scoutmaster.
  • 🔪 Pocket Knife: Scouts with their Totin' Chip may carry a knife. Ask your Scoutmaster if you need to earn yours first!
  • ☀️ Sunscreen: April sun in NC is strong — sun protection matters during outdoor activities.
  • 🦟 Bug Spray: Ticks are very active in NC in spring. Use DEET-based spray and check for ticks morning and evening.

✅ Nice-to-Have Items:

  • 🎒 Small day pack for hikes and activities
  • 📚 Scout handbook and rank advancement materials
  • 🌂 Small umbrella (backup for rain)
  • 🧻 Personal pack of tissues

❌ Leave at Home:

  • Video game systems, tablets (leave valuables at home)
  • Cell phones — Scouts do not need them on this trip
  • Deodorant with strong fragrance (attracts wildlife)
  • Expensive jewelry or items you'd be heartbroken to lose
 🎒 Part 4: Packing for the Trailer

🚛 How Gear Gets to Camp: All gear travels in the Troop trailer — not in family cars. That means everything must fit in one duffel bag or large backpack, plus one small daypack. This keeps the trailer organized and ensures every Scout's gear arrives together.

📦 Suggested Packing Strategy:

  • Main duffel or backpack: Sleeping bag (in its stuff sack), sleeping pad (rolled or folded), camp pillow (compressed), clothing, and personal items.
  • Small daypack: Items you'll want quick access to — headlamp, snacks, water bottle, rain jacket, sunscreen, bug spray, first aid, Scout handbook.
  • Snacks: Pack snacks in a labeled zip-lock inside your daypack.

🏷️ Label Everything — Yourself: Write your own name on every bag, stuff sack, and piece of gear before it goes in the trailer. Don't ask a parent to do this — it's your gear and your responsibility. Trailer mix-ups happen!

⏰ Practice First: If you've never set up your tent, practice in the backyard once before the trip — you'll thank yourself!
